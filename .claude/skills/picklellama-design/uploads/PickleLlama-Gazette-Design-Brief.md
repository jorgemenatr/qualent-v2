# The Pickle Llama Gazette — Design Brief

**PickleLlama Studio · Brand content program · v0.2 · September 2026**

---

## 1. Purpose of this document

This brief hands the Pickle Llama content concept to design. It defines the character, the editorial formats, the channels, and what the design system needs to produce so that articles can be written and published at a sustainable cadence without the character drifting or every post being designed from scratch.

**What we need from design:**

1. A character pose library (vector) that covers every recurring scene.
2. A small set of layout templates, one per editorial format, per channel.
3. A newsletter masthead and page system.
4. Extensions to the existing PickleLlama design system (palette, type, iconography) where the Gazette needs them.

**What is already decided:** the character, his voice, the formats, the guardrails, and the visual canon. Design should extend these, not reinterpret them. Where something is open, it is listed in Section 9.

---

## 2. The concept in one paragraph

PickleLlama publishes satirical news about its mascot, the Pickle Llama, in the style of *The Onion*. The Gazette reports his exploits deadpan, as straight news. The world he moves through is the world of our ideal clients: family-owned manufacturers, freight companies, construction firms, and distributors running on spreadsheets, paper, and an ERP rollout that never ends. The joke is never at the client's expense. The targets are the vendors, consultants, committees, and spreadsheets that make their lives harder. The Pickle Llama is the only competent, sane figure in a dysfunctional world, and the world treats him as the strange one. Every joke connects to a real operational pain. The sales message is the punchline, never the pitch.

**Why this works for an AI-augmented consultancy:** the content is openly AI-assisted about an AI-generated character. Satire is specific by definition, and specificity is what separates this from generic "AI content." The editorial voice is human-owned; production is where the tooling helps.

## 3. Character bible

### 3.1 Name
**The Pickle Llama.** Definite article, always. No nickname, no first name. Referred to as "the Pickle Llama" on first mention and "the Llama" thereafter in body copy.

### 3.2 Origin (canon)
An AI-generated hybrid of a pickle and a llama. He knows this and does not find it troubling. Nobody knows what he was before the image was generated; he declines to discuss it.

### 3.3 Archetype
The competent absurdist. The only sane one in an insane world, treated by that world as the crazy one. Reference points: Ron Swanson's competence and contempt for bureaucracy; The Stig's mystery (he appears in reports *about* him, rarely on camera); a llama's natural, withering stare. He is not a lovable screw-up. Competence is the brand. All his flaws are social.

### 3.4 What he is good at
- Shipping working software the same day. His defining, near-supernatural ability. The world finds it suspicious.
- Opening the Access database nobody else can open. Reading the 41-tab workbook that runs the company. Finding the one macro Gary wrote in 2009.
- Saying no. To scope, to Phase Two, to the steering committee.
- Eating. He is chewing through most scenes.

### 3.5 What he is bad at
- Meetings. He cannot remain in a room where a status update is happening. Has left mid-sentence. Has left mid-*his own* sentence.
- Diplomacy. Llamas spit. He spits at bad ideas. There have been incidents.
- Corporate ritual: the offsite, the all-hands, the vision deck, the vendor golf day.
- Explaining himself. He answers every question in under seven words.

### 3.6 Movement
No fixed address. Datelines are always somewhere new and oddly specific: a job site trailer outside Red Deer, a lumber yard in Saltillo, a dispatch office in Hamilton at 5:40 a.m. Nobody sees him arrive. Staff frequently report he was "already there when they opened the building." The Gazette has tried and failed to determine how he travels.

### 3.7 Speech
Near-silent. He is quoted sparingly, usually as a one-line answer to a long question from a reporter. Terse, declarative, faintly menacing. Never uses an exclamation point. Never explains a joke. Never sells.

Canonical examples:
- Asked how he opened the Access database: "I clicked it."
- Asked about Phase Two: "There is no Phase Two."
- Asked whether the spreadsheet could be replaced: "Yes." He then left.
- "It was a spreadsheet. Now it isn't."
- "Ask your boss."

### 3.8 His enemies (the satirical targets)
The Spreadsheet is the arch-enemy. Around it, a recurring cast:

| Villain | What it is | How the Gazette treats it |
|---|---|---|
| **The Spreadsheet Lobby** | Shadowy, well-funded organization | Subject of investigations and political attack ads |
| **Phase Two** | The ERP rollout that never ends | Covered like a war or a slow geological event |
| **The Transformation Consultant** | Bills by the hour, produces decks, has never opened the actual system | Earnest quotes, always in a blazer |
| **The Vendor** | Quotes 18 months and $600K, mostly "discovery" | Press releases the Gazette reprints without comment |
| **The Steering Committee** | A body that steers nothing | Meeting minutes, reported straight |
| **The Blame-Shifting Exec** | Takes credit, passes blame to the team | The one human villain; always unnamed |

These map directly onto our ICP disqualifiers. The satire pre-qualifies the audience: leaders who care about their team laugh at these villains; leaders who *are* these villains scroll past.

### 3.9 His heroes (never the butt of the joke)
The people drowning in the system. The Llama is on their side, often the only one who is. Readers should recognize themselves as the person he shows up for.

- **The Next-Gen Owner** — just took over the family business, discovering what it actually runs on.
- **The Dispatcher** — holding a logistics company together with a workbook and a group chat.
- **The Controller Who Knows Where Everything Is** — the only person who can find the file.
- **The Plant Manager** — has a clipboard, a radio, and no time.

### 3.10 Guardrails (non-negotiable)
1. Never punch down at the client persona.
2. Never name or recognizably parody a real company or person. Villains are generic archetypes.
3. Competence is never the joke. If a bit requires him to fail at software, it is wrong.
4. Every joke connects to a real operational pain. If you cannot name the pain, cut it.
5. Irreverent toward institutions, never toward people. He spits at PowerPoint, not at the intern.
6. He never sells. "Ask your boss about PickleLlama" is the only CTA he is permitted, and it is always fine print.
7. No exclamation points in his mouth. Ever.

## 4. Editorial voice

There are two voices, and design should be able to tell them apart at a glance.

**The Gazette (the narrator).** Deadpan, AP-style, earnest. Reports his exploits as straight news. Never winks. The comedy lives entirely in the gap between the seriousness of the reporting and the absurdity of the event. This voice does most of the work and should *look* like a real newspaper: datelines, bylines, kicker text, a masthead, section labels.

**The Llama (quoted).** Rare, short, set apart. Pull quotes are the natural device.

## 5. Editorial formats

Each format is a recurring container. Design needs one template per format (see Section 7).

| Format | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Straight news** | Headline + dateline + 80–150 word report. The core unit. | *Area Distributor's ERP Rollout Enters Its Fourteenth Year of Phase Two* |
| **Ad break: Pharmaceutical** | Fake drug ad. Side-effects language, "ask your doctor" structure. Uses the lab-coat Llama. | "PickleLlama Automation. Side effects may include weekends." |
| **Ad break: Injury lawyer** | "You may be entitled to…" | "Injured by a spreadsheet? You may be entitled to free time." |
| **Ad break: Political** | Attack ads paid for by the Spreadsheet Lobby; campaign ads for the Llama. | "Read my lips: no more spreadsheets." |
| **Ad break: PSA / animal-rights** | PETA-style. PickleLlama Farms. Free-range software. | "Every year, millions of spreadsheets are subjected to VLOOKUP." |
| **Investigation** | Longer, multi-part, faux-serious. | *Who Is Really Funding Phase Two?* |
| **Letters to the editor** | Readers write in with spreadsheet horror stories. The Llama answers in seven words or fewer. Becomes user-generated over time. | — |
| **Classifieds** | Tiny, dense, deadpan. | "WANTED: anyone who knows the password to the shared drive." |
| **Archival photo** | The original photo-real render, reprinted as a grainy "rare photograph." | "The Pickle Llama, believed to be photographed in 2023." |
| **The real column** | A short piece in John's actual voice. No character. The only non-satirical section. | — |

Existing house "advertisers," both with finished art: **PickleLlama Farms** (free-range software) and the **PickleLlama Automation** prescription/nutrition label.

## 6. Channels

| Channel | Role | Primary formats | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Where decision-makers are. Where the sales message lands. | Straight news, investigations, the real column | 2× / week |
| **Instagram** | Where the people who go tell their boss are. Image-first, shareable without context. | Ad breaks, archival photos, "day in the life," classifieds as single tiles | 2–3× / week, mirrored from LinkedIn where sensible |
| **Newsletter** | The full paper. The place people eventually reply. | Lead story + one ad break + one letter + the real column | Biweekly |

## 7. What design needs to produce

### 7.1 Character pose library (vector)
Six poses minimum, in the established flat vector style (see Section 8). Each pose is a standalone asset composited onto illustrated or generated backgrounds. This is the recipe already proven on the mug designs and it is the recipe for everything.

| # | Pose | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Standing, neutral stare (front and three-quarter) | Default; news cards |
| 2 | Staring at a monitor / laptop | Database, spreadsheet stories |
| 3 | Chewing | Any scene where he is present but uninvolved |
| 4 | Walking away, mid-stride, back partly turned | Leaving meetings |
| 5 | Mid-spit (restrained, implied) | Reacting to bad ideas |
| 6 | Sitting in a truck / on a forklift / in a job trailer | Datelines, "already there" bits |

Plus the two costumes:
- **Default** (nothing).
- **Lab coat with PL badge** — exclusively for pharma/lab ad breaks. Already exists.

Optional additions once the base six are done: holding a clipboard; standing at a podium (political ads); silhouette-only (investigation art, "sighting" stories).

### 7.2 Layout templates
One per format, at minimum for the three channels. Priority order:

1. **News card** (LinkedIn 1200×627 and 1080×1080; Instagram 1080×1080 and 1080×1350). Masthead strip, section kicker, headline, dateline, character + background scene. Must work with headline lengths of 8–20 words.
2. **Ad break: Pharma** — extend the existing prescription/nutrition-label system to a square and portrait format.
3. **Ad break: PSA / Farms** — extend the existing Farms label to square and portrait.
4. **Ad break: Political** — new. Attack-ad grammar: harsh crop, black-and-white or desaturated photo treatment, "Paid for by the Spreadsheet Lobby" disclaimer band.
5. **Ad break: Injury lawyer** — new. Bus-bench / late-night-TV grammar. Phone-number-style CTA that just says "Ask your boss."
6. **Letters to the editor** — text-led, small type, two-column newspaper feel. The Llama's seven-word reply set in a distinct style.
7. **Classifieds** — dense, monospaced or condensed, single tile.
8. **Archival photo** — frame treatment for the original render: grain, caption, "photo undated."
9. **Investigation** — multi-tile carousel (Instagram) / long post header (LinkedIn).

Each template needs a light and a dark variant only if the channel demands it; default is the existing off-white background.

### 7.3 Newsletter system
- **Masthead** for *The Pickle Llama Gazette*. Newspaper nameplate conventions: rules, edition line, date, "Est." line (suggest "Est. 2023, Reprinted Under Protest"), price line (suggest "Price: One Spreadsheet").
- **Page structure:** lead story → ad break → letters → the real column → footer with "Ask your boss about PickleLlama" as fine print.
- **Section labels** as a reusable type style: NEWS, INVESTIGATION, LETTERS, CLASSIFIEDS, ADVERTISEMENT, FROM THE DESK OF (the real column).
- Email-safe: single column, web fonts with fallbacks, images with alt text, works in dark-mode clients.

### 7.4 Design-system extensions
- **Newspaper typographic layer.** The existing brand is display-heavy (bold rounded wordmark, geometric sans). The Gazette needs a "reporting" register: a serif or newspaper-grotesque for headlines and body that reads as real news, sitting inside the existing brand frame. The contrast between the earnest newspaper type and the cartoon llama is part of the joke.
- **Villain iconography.** Simple line icons for the recurring villains (spreadsheet grid, "Phase Two" badge, deck/slides, committee table, lobby seal). Same weight as the existing "organic automation / ethically sourced" icon set.
- **Dateline / byline / kicker components.**
- **Disclaimer band** component, reused across all ad breaks ("Paid for by…", "Side effects may include…", "Ask your boss about PickleLlama").

## 8. Existing assets and visual canon

**The vector mascot is the character for all content.** Green pickle body with dark ink line work and short spines, grey-white llama hindquarters and legs, black dot eyes, small llama muzzle, curled stem on top, slight default smile and nothing more. This is the only version of him that appears in new content.

**The original photo-real render is archival only.** It is never the working character. It appears exclusively as the "rare photograph" bit.

**Existing assets to work from:**
- `original-picklellama.jpeg` — the original AI render (archival use only)
- `llama-left.svg` — vector mascot, standing, facing left
- `logo.svg` — lab-coat variant with PL badge
- `logo-text.png` — wordmark
- `MugDesing-04.jpg` — Automation Facts nutrition-label ad (pharma / label voice)
- `MugDesing-05.jpg` — PickleLlama Farms free-range software ad (PSA / animal-rights voice)
- Existing brand palette: lime green (wordmark and accents), off-white background, near-black display type, the darker olive of the wordmark. Sample exact values from the assets.

The two mug designs are the reference for tone, density, and how the character is composited onto scenes. Everything in the Gazette should feel like it came from the same shop.

## 9. Open questions for design

1. **Newspaper type.** Serif or newspaper-grotesque for the reporting register? Pick two candidates and show them against the wordmark.
2. **Masthead name.** *The Pickle Llama Gazette* is the working title. Alternatives considered: *The Daily Llama*, *The Brine Times*. Design may propose based on what sets best.
3. **Villain rendering.** Are villains ever drawn, or only described? Proposal: villains are never drawn as characters, only as objects and icons (a spreadsheet, a deck, an empty committee table). This keeps the Llama the only character and avoids the cast expanding.
4. **Background production.** Illustrated backgrounds (consistent, slower) vs. generated backgrounds in the mug-design style (faster, needs a locked style reference). Recommendation: lock one generated style that matches the Farms mug background and reuse it.
5. **Instagram portrait vs. square.** Do we commit to one aspect ratio to halve the template count?

## Appendix B — Sample article (voice reference)

**Logistics Firm Hires Fourth Dispatcher to Manage Spreadsheet That Tracks the Other Three**

*HAMILTON, ONT.* — A regional freight company confirmed Tuesday that its newest dispatcher will be responsible for maintaining the spreadsheet that tracks what the other three dispatchers are doing, a role management described as "critical." The workbook, which reportedly contains 41 tabs and one macro nobody will touch, is opened by all four employees simultaneously each morning, at which point it asks whether they want to save changes. The Pickle Llama, who was observed in the dispatch office before it opened, declined to comment on the hire. Asked whether the spreadsheet could be replaced, he said, "Yes." He then left.

*Sponsored: PickleLlama Automation. Side effects may include weekends. Ask your boss about PickleLlama.*
