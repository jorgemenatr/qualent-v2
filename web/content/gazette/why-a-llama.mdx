This is the one part of the paper that isn’t a bit, so I’ll be straight with you.

The Pickle Llama is AI-generated. He was generated as a joke, and then he stuck, and now he is the most recognisable thing about this company. We are openly an AI-augmented consultancy writing openly AI-assisted satire about an openly AI-generated mascot. Pretending otherwise would be the only dishonest move available to us.

Here is why it isn’t lazy. Satire has to be specific or it isn’t funny. You cannot write a joke about a 41-tab workbook unless you have actually sat with someone while they opened it and it asked whether they wanted to save changes. Every headline in this paper maps to something we have seen in a real business. The editorial judgement — what is funny, what is true, what would embarrass a client — is mine. The production is where the tooling helps.

And there’s a filter in it. The villains in this paper are the vendor, the committee, the consultant who has never opened the system, and the executive who passes blame down. If those are your enemies too, we will probably get along. If you recognise yourself in them, you will scroll past, and that saves us both a meeting.

The people in this paper who are never the joke are the dispatcher, the controller who knows where the file is, the plant manager with a clipboard and no time, and the owner who just took over and is finding out what the place actually runs on. The Llama shows up for them. So do we. He’s just faster, and he doesn’t do meetings.
